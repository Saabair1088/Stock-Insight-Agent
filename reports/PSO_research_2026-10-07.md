## 4. Bull versus bear debate

**Bull case.**

1. **Valuation is the anchor.** 0.58x book (BVPS 592.43), 6.6x consolidated earnings, 4.7x EV/EBITDA and a 15% earnings yield. You are buying the country's fuel logistics network for less than the value of its inventory, storage and refinery stake.
2. **The Q4 loss is a trading artefact, not a broken business.** Gross profit excluding LNG still rose 20.5% to Rs 81.9bn in FY26; the ~Rs 23bn Q4 hit came from marking down inventory as prices fell, and it reverses when prices stabilise.
3. **Receivables are finally moving the right way:** Rs 437.5bn to Rs 414.8bn, SNGPL down Rs 34.3bn, and finance costs down 24% as rates ease. Every rupee collected is a rupee of book value released.
4. **Integration optionality:** 63.6% of PRL (upgrading to 100,000 bpd), a stake in the Pak-Arab pipeline, and the SOCAR/FWO Faisalabad-Tarujabba white-oil pipeline - each one narrows the gap between a 0.58x book multiple and replacement cost.
5. **The street is not subtle about it:** six analysts, Strong Buy, Rs 697 target (+96%), and a dividend that has been Rs 10/share for four years running with an 18% payout ratio.


**Bear case.**

1. **The core is shrinking.** Motor-gasoline volumes -5%, diesel -6%, white-oil share down to ~42.7% as smaller OMCs undercut PSO. A 0.58x book multiple is what a market pays for a melting franchise, not necessarily a mispricing.
2. **LNG is a structural leak.** Six cargoes in Q4 against 28 a year earlier and a Rs 10.7bn annual swing - with take-or-pay obligations that do not care about demand.
3. **Earnings quality is poor.** FY26 standalone profit fell 28% and the group's +55% came from subsidiaries and associates; a 60% effective tax rate means the state takes more of the pre-tax profit than shareholders do.
4. **Circular debt is the real balance-sheet story:** Rs 415bn owed, largely by government gas and power entities. PSO is a lender of last resort to the state, and it has been for a decade.
5. **The chart is still broken.** The stock is 30% below its 52-week high and below its 200-day average, in a market where politics has been in charge for a quarter - and it is a quarter of the index.

## 12. Data and method caveats

- The model applies the full dividend as a step down on the ex-date, which is the textbook treatment; PSX high-yield names often give back less than the full amount on the day, so treat the price median as the conservative case and the total-return probability as the decision number.
- Yahoo's PSO feed lags by up to two sessions: the 6-7 Oct 2026 closes are taken from stockanalysis.com and cross-checked against sarmaaya.pk's 8 Oct quote (previous close 355.86).
- The daily series contains a handful of padded rows for PSX holidays (zero-change closes); they are de-duplicated on ingest and the effect on volatility is negligible.
- The long-term moving-average reference is proxied by the 10-month moving average of the 5-year monthly series because the environment's daily history starts in January 2026; the proxy (PKR ~377) sits within 1.5% of the 200-day average reported by stockanalysis (382.59).
- The committee is the deterministic mirror of the TradingAgents pipeline (same roles, same data
  tools, same rating scale); when an LLM key is available the vendored graph can be run instead.
- Probabilities are model output, not promises. The scenario book is analyst judgement and is
  stated so it can be scored later.
