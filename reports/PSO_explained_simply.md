# PSO explained simply
### Pakistan State Oil — what our research actually says, in normal words

*Prepared 2026-10-07 · share price used: Rs 355.86 (the close on Wednesday 7 October 2026)*

The full technical report is `PSO_research_2026-10-07.md` in this folder. This page is the
same conclusion written for someone who does not work in a bank.

---

## 1. First, two things people need to know

**What is PSO?** Pakistan State Oil is the country's biggest fuel company. If you buy petrol,
diesel, jet fuel or lubricants in Pakistan, there is a good chance PSO sold it. It also imports
LNG (the gas used to run power stations), runs about 3,500-plus petrol pumps, stores over a
million tonnes of fuel, and owns 63.6% of Pakistan Refinery Limited. It is partly owned by the
government (about 22.5% directly, with state institutions holding more), so it is treated as a
national asset rather than just another company.

**What are we being asked?** Two questions:

1. Is this a good business to own right now? (the "deep research")
2. Where is the share price likely to be in about a month — around **6 November 2026**? (the
   "30-day prediction")

---

## 2. The short answer — the verdict in one line

> **It's a Hold.** We think a shareholder is slightly more likely to end the month ahead than
> behind — a **56%** chance of a positive *total* return — but the share price itself is likely
> to print **lower**, around **Rs 349**, because Rs 10 of cash leaves the company on 20 October.
> A realistic range for the share price on 6 November is roughly **Rs 324 to Rs 374**.

That sounds like a contradiction ("more likely up, but lower?"), and section 3 explains exactly
why. It is the single most important thing on this page.

---

## 3. The dividend: why the share price is expected to *fall* even though you make money

PSO is paying a dividend of **Rs 10 per share** for the year ended June 2026. The important date
is **20 October 2026** — the "ex-dividend date".

On that morning, anyone buying the share no longer gets the Rs 10. So the share price is
expected to open roughly Rs 10 lower — about **2.8%** — with nothing actually going wrong.
You still own the same company and you will be paid Rs 10 in cash on 19 November.

Think of it like this: your wallet has Rs 355 in it. On 20 October, Rs 10 moves from your right
pocket to your left pocket. You are not richer and you are not poorer; you simply own a bit more
cash and a slightly cheaper share.

So there are two honest questions, and we answer both:

| Question | Our answer |
|---|---|
| Will the **printed share price** be higher on 6 November than Rs 355.86? | **35%** chance — likely no |
| Will a **shareholder be ahead** (share price change + the Rs 10 cash)? | **56%** chance — slightly likely yes |

The second line is the one that matters to a real investor. The first line is the one that will
appear in the newspaper.

## 5. Why the share looks so cheap

1. **You are buying fuel infrastructure at a discount to its recorded value.** Rs 592 of net
   assets per share is sitting on the books; the market pays Rs 356 for it.
2. **The core business outside LNG is still growing.** Gross profit excluding LNG rose 20.5% last
   year to Rs 82bn. Fuel volumes are softer, but margins per litre improved.
3. **The money owed to PSO is finally shrinking.** The company is owed Rs 415bn — down from
   Rs 437bn — and the gas utility SNGPL alone paid back Rs 34bn more than it added. Interest
   costs fell 24% as rates eased.
4. **It is the country's fuel lifeline.** 99% of Pakistan's jet fuel, ~42% of white-oil sales,
   1.23 million tonnes of storage and a refinery stake. Businesses like this rarely disappear.
5. **The dividend has been Rs 10 for four years running**, paid in cash, with only an 18% payout
   ratio — so it is not stretching the company to pay it.

---

## 6. Why the market refuses to pay up (the honest bad news)

1. **RS 415 BILLION IS STUCK.** This is the whole story. Most of that money is owed by government
   gas and power companies. PSO is effectively lending to the state, year after year. Until that
   cash comes back — or turns into something PSO owns — the market will keep valuing PSO as a
   risky lender rather than a fuel company. (People call this "circular debt".)
2. **Last year's profit fell 28%** to Rs 15.07bn, and the final quarter (April–June 2026) was a
   loss of roughly **Rs 23bn**. Reason: oil prices fell after a spike, and the fuel sitting in
   PSO's tanks was suddenly worth less. Inventory losses like this can repeat any quarter prices
   move sharply.
3. **The LNG business collapsed.** PSO handled 6 LNG cargoes in the quarter against 28 a year
   earlier. That alone swings around Rs 10.7bn a year.
4. **People are buying less fuel.** Petrol volumes fell about 5% and diesel about 6% over the
   year, and PSO's share of white-oil sales slipped to about 42.7% as smaller competitors grabbed
   business with sharper prices.
5. **The chart is still broken.** The shares are roughly 30% below their 52-week high of Rs 507
   and below their long-term average (near Rs 377–383), in a market where politics has been the
   main driver for months.

## 8. The trade, if you are going to do one

Our committee's plan, in plain words:

* **Don't chase.** Buy on weakness toward Rs 335–345 rather than paying up above Rs 363.
* **The line in the sand is Rs 322.** If PSO closes below that (about 1.5× its normal daily
  swing below the Rs 337 support shelf), the thesis is wrong and the position should go.
* **Upside markers: Rs 383 (first) and Rs 434 (second).** Rs 383 is the long-term average that
  has capped every rally this year.
* **Size it modestly** — about 6% of a diversified portfolio. This is a 21%-a-year-volatility
  share in a market where politics is in charge; it can move against you fast.
* Over the next month the model puts about **15% odds on the price touching Rs 322 at some point**,
  and about **9% odds of actually finishing below it** on 6 November.

## 10. What would prove us wrong

* **Bullish surprise:** a weekly close above **Rs 383**, plus a Q1 FY27 that shows no fresh
  inventory loss — that flips the trend and would make us buyers rather than holders.
* **Bearish surprise:** Q1 FY27 showing a second consecutive quarterly loss, another quarter of
  6–8 LNG cargoes, or a 26 October rate hike into a falling market.
* **The wildcard:** an actual circular-debt settlement. Nothing else would change PSO's story
  as much, and nothing else is as unpredictable.

## 11. Being straight about the limits of this

* This is a **probabilistic** forecast, not a promise. A 56% chance of being up means roughly
  one time in two we expect to be wrong about the direction.
* The model applies the **full** Rs 10 dividend as a price step on 20 October, which is the
  textbook treatment. In practice, thinly traded high-yield shares on the PSX often fall by a
  little less than the full dividend — if that happens here, the price numbers above are the
  conservative case.
* We used PSO's **own 2026 daily trading history** (192 sessions) plus five years of monthly
  prices, checked against two independent sources. Prices are settled exchange closes, not
  live quotes.
* Nobody can model politics, a surprise LNG cargo decision, or a sharp oil move. Those are
  exactly the things that have driven this share for the past year.
* This is research, **not investment advice**. Position sizes assume a diversified portfolio.
