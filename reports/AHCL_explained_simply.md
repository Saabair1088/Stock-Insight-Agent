## 1. First, two things people need to know

**What is AHCL?** Arif Habib Corporation is a **holding company** — the simplest way to picture
it is a bag that owns pieces of several other businesses:

| What the bag contains | Share held | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **PIA (Pakistan International Airlines)** | ~34% of the consortium vehicle (with Fatima Fertilizer) that owns **100%** of the airline | The national airline — 17–18 planes in service, plans for 38 |
| **Arif Habib Limited** | ~73% | Pakistan's best-known brokerage and investment bank |
| **Sachal Energy** | ~86% | A wind-power plant |
| **Fatima Fertilizer** | ~15% | One of Pakistan's big fertilizer makers |
| **Power Cement, Aisha Steel, Javedan, Pakarab, Safe Mix** | smaller stakes | Cement, steel, real estate, fertilizer |

So AHCL is not a factory or an airline itself. It is an owner of other companies, and it collects
dividends from them.

**What are we being asked?** Two questions:

1. Is this a good business to own right now? (the "deep research")
2. Where is the share price likely to be in about a month — around **6 November 2026**? (the
   "30-day prediction")

## 3. The 8% dividend — read this before you buy for the "yield"

AHCL pays **Rs 1.25 per share**, a **8.1% yield** at today's price. On paper that looks wonderful.
But there is a catch that trips up a lot of people:

* **20 October 2026 is the ex-dividend date.** On that morning the share trades without the right
  to the dividend, so the price is expected to open about **8% lower** (roughly Rs 1.25).
* The cash is paid out on **19 November 2026**.

Nothing is lost — Rs 15.48 of share simply becomes about Rs 14.23 of share + Rs 1.25 of cash.
But it means:

* If you buy on **19 October**, you get the Rs 1.25.
* If you buy on **20 October**, you do not — but you pay roughly 8% less for the same business.

An 8% cash payment in one month is genuinely generous. It is the main reason this stock is worth
holding rather than avoiding. It is *not* free money, and that is why our forecast for the
printed price is lower while our forecast for the shareholder is flat-to-slightly-up.

## 5. The big new thing: AHCL now owns the national airline

This is why the stock matters again.

* In **December 2025**, an Arif Habib-led consortium won the auction for a **75% stake in PIA**
  with a bid of **Rs 135 billion** (the government's minimum was Rs 100bn). The airline was
  valued at Rs 180 billion.
* The government kept 25% and received about Rs 10bn in cash; **roughly Rs 125 billion is to be
  injected into the airline itself** — new engines, maintenance, expansion.
* In **April 2026** the consortium bought the remaining 25% for **Rs 45 billion**, so it now owns
  **100%** of PIA.
* The airline came with about **Rs 54 billion of liabilities**, 17–18 flyable aircraft, and a plan
  to grow to 38 aircraft. All employees were retained for at least 12 months.
* The buying vehicle is a company called **PIA Equity Ltd**, whose owners include AHCL, Fatima
  Fertilizer, Fauji Fertilizer, Lake City, AKD and The City School. AHCL and Fatima's combined
  share is about **34%**.

**Why this is exciting:** PIA has been a drain on taxpayers for decades. If a private owner can
make an airline work in a country of 250 million people, AHCL's stake could be worth many times
what was paid. This is a genuine "option" on a turnaround.

**Why this is dangerous:** Rs 125 billion is a lot of money for a group whose own market value is
Rs 65 billion. Airlines are expensive, fuel-hungry businesses — fuel alone was quoted near
$101–103 a barrel in early October. If PIA's losses outrun the plan, the money has to come from
somewhere: more borrowing, or asking shareholders for more cash.

## 6. The rest of the business (the part that actually pays the dividend)

* **Arif Habib Limited** (the brokerage, ~73% owned) had its best year: profit Rs 1.18bn, and it
  paid out Rs 12.50 per share in dividends.
* **Sachal Energy** (wind power, ~86% owned) paid AHCL Rs 1.37bn.
* **Fatima Fertilizer** (~15% owned) paid AHCL Rs 2.29bn.
* These cash flows are what fund the 8% dividend AHCL pays you. Watch them — if they slow down,
  the dividend slows down.

**The wobble:** because AHCL is a holding company, its profits swing with the stock market. When
the KSE-100 index falls, AHCL's own trading portfolio takes a paper loss. In January–March 2026
that produced a **Rs 8.4 billion loss** — the market fell, not the businesses. The full-year
number still came out at Rs 15.53bn profit (+49.5%) because the rest of the year was strong.

## 8. The trade, if you are going to do one

* **Treat the dividend as the reason to own it**, not the momentum. Collecting Rs 1.25 in cash in
  November is a real return; the price trend is not your friend.
* **The line in the sand is Rs 13.57.** Below that the shares are back to roughly book value with
  the PIA risk still attached.
* **Upside markers: Rs 16.55 and Rs 17.00.** Both have capped the price repeatedly.
* **Keep it small** — about 5–6% of a diversified portfolio. This is a 24%-volatility stock with
  only 15% of its shares trading freely, which means the price can jump or sag for no good reason.
* The model puts about **5–6% odds on touching the Rs 13.57 stop** during the month, and around
  **2% odds on touching Rs 17.00**.

## 10. What would prove us wrong

* **Bullish surprise:** PIA's first consolidated quarter looks under control, an operational
  milestone lands (planes back in service, a route restored, talk of re-listing), or the company
  does something that narrows the gap between the value of its pieces and its own share price
  (a buyback, a special dividend).
* **Bearish surprise:** Q1 FY27 showing PIA's losses dragging group earnings into the red; any
  request for fresh money from shareholders; or a cut in the Fatima / AHL dividends that fund
  AHCL's own payout.
* **Watch the index too:** a KSE-100 close below 160,000 hits AHCL's portfolio marks directly.

## 11. Being straight about the limits of this

* **The price history for this share is thin.** Yahoo Finance has no feed for AHCL at all, so our
  daily data covers 50 trading sessions (28 July – 7 October 2026) from one source, cross-checked
  against two others. That is enough to measure today's volatility, but not enough to describe
  rare events — so we also ran a fat-tail cross-check and reported both.
* **No analyst covers AHCL**, so there is no consensus target to anchor the price. Anyone quoting
  one is guessing.
* The model applies the **full** Rs 1.25 as a price step on the ex-date. In practice, high-yield
  shares often fall a little less than the full dividend — if that happens, the numbers above are
  the conservative case.
* **Rumours about this share are rife** (suspension notices, takeovers, PIA stories). We only used
  filings, press coverage with dates, and exchange data; where the sources disagreed we said so.
* This is research, **not investment advice**. Position sizes assume a diversified portfolio.
